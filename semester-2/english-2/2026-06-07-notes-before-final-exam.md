## Presentation Skills

- What's Makes Presentation Good?
  - Clear Structure: Intro -> Body (Main Content) -> Conclusion
  - Logical Order: Ideas Connect naturally and supporting each order
  - Evidence-based: Ideas/Claim/Argument are supported by credible sources
    like cited academic sources (Paper/Journal/Textbook)
  - Know your Audience: language and depth are adjusted for the audience's knowledge level.
  - Effective Visuals/Slide: Slides reinforces what speaker want to say.
- Structure
  - Introduction: Hook, background, purpose
  - Body, the main content: Arguments supported by evidence/data/facts
  - Conclusion: Summary and key takeaway
- Delivery Technique
  - Speak clearly & simply <- Help audience know what you're talking about
  - Maintain eye contact
  - Cite academic sources (journal, papers, books). Credit the author in slides/script.
  - Visual element (pictures, diagra, chart) <- image  worth 1000x than  words
  - Practice Thoroughly <- build fluency and confident
- Common Mistakes
  - Reading slides too much <- indicate that you dont really understand whats your point. Maintain your eye contact and use simple language
  - Too much text on slides <- overwhelm users
  - Unreliable or uncited sources (wikipedia, blogs, social media post) <- need effort to verify
  - speaking too fast
  - poor time management
- Academic Arguments
  - Golden rule: No opinion/claim without evidence.
  - Acceptable sources:
    - Academic journal (IEEE, Scopus, ACM)
    - Textbooks and published book
    - Conferences procedings

## Writing Skills

- Academic writing essentials:
  - Purpose: explain, analyze, persuade, evaluate, report.
  - Audience: public/layperson who not shared same background with you. So, write it crystal clear, don't use too much jargon.
  - Evidence: every ideas/claims/arguments are supported by data, expert views, facts, or research.
  - Style: formal, precise, objective, less personal.
  - Process: pre-write (problem finding), research, outline, draft, edit, rewrite.
- Process Writing Tips:
  - The best ess ays usually improve through repeated editing and rewriting.
  - Practical habit: write early → leave time → revise for argument, sources, structure, and language
- The Paragraph:
  - Basic building block.
  - One paragraph = one idea = one focused topic.
  - PEEL:
    - Point: (bold) statement of main idea. <- Writer's Position
    - Evidence: Use research, data, examples, or expert opinion.
    - Explain/Evaluate: show how evidence support the statement.
    - Link: Connect back to the thesis or next paragraph.
    Tip: most weak paragraphs have evidence, but not enough explanation/evaluation.
